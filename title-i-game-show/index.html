Title I Game Show
GRPS State and Federal Programs. All challenge data and plan excerpts are simulated.

Host guide

35–45 minutes. Teams of 3–5. Project this game from one computer. The host runs the screen. Teams discuss and answer aloud or hold up Fact / Fiction on scrap paper. No participant devices or additional forms are needed.

Choose a game selects 12 challenges: Game 1 Getting started, Game 2 Scholar support, Game 3 Family engagement, Game 4 Instruction and support, or Game 5 Planning and review. Customize the rounds offers optional separate versions for each round. Team count only changes the scoreboard. Start a new game applies the selected sets; Resume continues the saved game. A game always contains 12 challenges: six Fact or Fiction statements, three plan repairs, and three questions to explain. There are 60 unique questions across all five versions. Choose 1, 2, 3, or 4 teams on the opening screen. Only the selected teams appear in scoring and final results. Rename them on the game screen. Scores and progress save only in this browser when browser storage is available. Do not enter scholar information.

Opening and exit question

Before round 1, teams answer one unscored question matched to the selected Fact or Fiction version. Record A, B, or C, or skip it. At the end, use Answer the exit question to repeat it and compare choices. Ask teams to explain their reasoning. The five pairs cover program models (1: A), scholar selection (2: B), the annual Title I meeting (3: C), staff learning impact (4: B), and needs before spending (5: A). Each exit question repeats the opening question from that game. The comparison shows the correct answer and explanation. This is a reflection on one idea, not a complete assessment. Round openings introduce each activity. Expand Why this matters when more explanation is useful.

The three rounds

  • Fact or Fiction, 10–12 minutes: six statements. Give all teams 30 seconds to choose, then invite a different team to explain each time. Reveal the explanation.
  • Fix This Plan, 12–15 minutes: three short excerpts. Give teams 60 seconds. Hear one correction from each team before the reveal.
  • Ask Title I, 8–10 minutes: three everyday questions. Give teams 30 seconds to prepare. Invite each team to give a brief answer, using different speakers.

Points and participation

Each question is worth two points. The reveal lists two criteria, worth one point each. Award each team 0, 1, or 2 using its buttons. Points measure the response on that question. Accept accurate wording different from the example. Speed earns no bonus. Invite quieter voices and rotate speakers.

Round 1 teams earn one point for the vote and one for a sound explanation. Ask teams you did not hear to share their explanation with another team. Award based on what they can explain. Scores are optional, and no team loses points for asking a question.

The timer is optional. It pauses, resumes, and resets. With the page focused, Space reveals an answer or advances to the next question. When a button has focus, Space activates that button. A timer ending does not advance the game. Read the explanation after every reveal and ask what it changes about the team’s understanding of Title I. Keep funding questions that require district review separate from game scoring.

Ending the game

Recognize the highest score or a tie. The final question is unscored: ask everyone to explain one Title I idea more clearly than they could at the start. Identify one section of the school plan worth revisiting in an existing meeting.

Template connections

The question reveals identify areas from the Title I Targeted and Schoolwide Program Requirements Template, version 11.06.2020.a. The game covers selected areas and does not replace the full plan. Questions use the template’s numbering.
